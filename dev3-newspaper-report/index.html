Full RappNews collection recovered locally

The full folio was still on disk. Its Harvest dataset registration was missing and its Ink publication was hidden. The generic restore command preferred an enhanced sample over the full archive. The full publication has now been explicitly unhidden at rappnews-iiif; the collection and 1953/2007 issue readers returned HTTP 200 on the local server.

The rebuilt archive contains 3,115 issues, 964,527 article records and 43,019 distinct scans. Article-to-page copies account for the much larger page-table row count. The full archive has completed the supported workflow rebuild from its durable capture, with local backups retained.

Which images and OCR are these?

The full collection uses the vendor’s original per-page PDFs through PDF Tools, not the assembled 1953 PDF used for the clipping experiments. The delivery also retains JP2 masters and ALTO XML. For the first page of 29 October 1953, the captured JP2 is 4,772 × 7,315 pixels and the vendor-PDF IIIF image is 4,774 × 7,316. Similar dimensions do not prove equal image quality; a same-page visual or OCR comparison is still required before asserting that JP2 improves recognition.

That delivery’s ALTO identifies ABBYY FineReader and Apex CoVantage processing in June 2024. It is a separate OCR baseline from the recent Tesseract text supplied for the Halloween clipping. Preserve both provenance records when evaluating replacement OCR.

Evidence for a re-OCR program—with resolution and quality controls

These samples demonstrate recoverable text errors in supplied OCR and substantial differences between recognition methods. They support investing in a measured re-OCR pipeline and a broader corpus audit. They do not yet establish that every page should be replaced, that every provider has poor OCR, or that a single model wins across the corpus.

PDF Tools · Pitts’ Theatre advertisement

The 512-long-edge preset reduces this tall advertisement to just 167 pixels wide. Gemini preserves the schedule, performers, 16c/35c prices and November 3–4–5 dates much more faithfully in this sample. OpenAI loses performers and sections and reads “Bury Lancaster”. Ministral changes Shirley Booth to “Shirley Blithe”, introduces November 9 and changes the final dates; its prose converts 16c/35c to $1.6/$3.5 even while transcription retains cents. All three nevertheless claim needs_high_res=false. Tesseract on the source-resolution crop is far better than on the thumbnail, although names and incidental lines remain noisy. This is strong evidence against trusting the routing flag alone for OCR quality.

Recommended next evidence for the re-OCR decision

  1. Sample by provider, newspaper, year, scan quality and layout: ordinary columns, advertisements, tables, damaged print and mixed fonts.
  2. Keep supplied OCR and its provenance. Align each candidate to image coordinates; compare against a human-reviewed reference using word/character error rates and named-entity, date and amount checks.
  3. Run full-resolution Tesseract and a dedicated vision transcription task on the same regions. Test source-text-assisted correction separately; do not silently treat corrected text as a faithful transcript.
  4. Price complete pages, including segmentation, failed requests, retries, review and storage. Set acceptance thresholds before deciding whether to replace, supplement or retain each provider’s text.

Current conclusion: there is measurable opportunity to improve newspaper text, and visual evidence that resolution and model choice can dominate the result. A corpus-wide re-OCR effort should preserve existing text, validate replacements and target quality—not merely rerun the same engine on thumbnails.
